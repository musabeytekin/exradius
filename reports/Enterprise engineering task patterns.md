# Enterprises hand agents upkeep, demand evidence

Enterprise engineering is mostly not feature work: across the surveys and studies that measure it, writing new product code is roughly 10–20% of an engineer's week, while maintenance in the broad sense (debugging, upgrades, patching, flaky tests, toil) takes 30–50% and the rest goes to coordination, review and hunting for context. Tickets are thin, often just a title, a description and a record identifier, so the real specification lives in logs, traces, production records and change history that the ticket only points to. The work happens behind TLS-inspecting proxies, private artifact mirrors, golden base images and expiring SSO-bound tokens, and in regulated firms every production change must carry five provable facts: an authorised request, a description and impact assessment, test evidence, approval by someone independent of the author, and a traceable deployment with a fallback. The 2024–2026 record of unattended agents fits this shape exactly: they succeed at scale on mechanical, oracle-checked work (Java and framework upgrades, library migrations, monolith splits, vulnerability autofixes), the independent productivity evidence on general work is flat to negative, human review is the cost that grows, and the documented disasters all involve an agent that could reach production credentials. For a harness like Exradius, this means the deciding features are not prompt quality but corporate-network plumbing, egress and credential isolation, a complete and exportable per-change evidence record, a strict author-only role for the agent, and results built to cut reviewer time. The prioritised list at the end ties each of these to the evidence and to where Exradius stands today.

## Feature work fills a fifth of the week; upkeep and investigation fill the rest

The most direct recent measurement is Microsoft's 2024 "Time Warp" study of 484 engineers, who reported about **11% of the week coding, 9% debugging, 5% on review and 12% in communication and meetings**. They wanted less time on debugging, support tickets and security and compliance, and time on security and compliance beyond their ideal was significantly associated with lower satisfaction ([arXiv 2502.15287](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15287v1)). Chainguard's vendor-funded 2025 survey of 1,200 engineers and leaders puts writing code and building features at **about 16% of the week**, with 79% naming code maintenance as a major drain ([Chainguard](https://www.chainguard.dev/2026-engineering-reality-report?gated=no)). Older self-estimates bracket broad maintenance at around 30% (Sonar, 2019) to over 17 hours a week (Stripe, 2018) ([Sonar](https://www.sonarsource.com/blog/developers-spend-30-of-their-time-on-code-maintenance-our-latest-survey-results-part-3/); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=541182)). No 2022–2026 source measures an enterprise-only split by task type, so the 30–50% maintenance band is an inference across surveys that do not use the same definitions. Still, every source points the same way.

Within that maintenance share, the recurring mechanical work is identifiable and partly measured. IDC's 2024 study for JFrog found developers estimating **about 19% of weekly hours on security tasks**, including 3.5 hours a week reviewing scan findings because of false positives and duplicates ([JFrog/IDC](https://s201.q4cdn.com/814780939/files/doc_news/2024/10/final_idc_devsecurity-pr_oct2024.pdf)). An industrial case study puts flaky tests at **at least 2.5% of productive developer time** ([ICST 2024](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icst-2024/icst-2024-industry/1/Cost-of-Flaky-Tests-in-CI-An-Industrial-Case-Study)). Amazon states a Java 17 upgrade averaged **about 50 developer-days per application** before automation ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/amazons-ai-assistant-saves-4500-years-of-development-time-ceo-andy-jassy-says/)). Google's SRE guidance caps toil at 50% of an SRE's time and found an average near 33%, mostly interrupts, on-call and releases ([Google SRE](https://sre.google/sre-book/eliminating-toil/)). What these categories share is that the input is machine-readable (a CVE id, a package and target version, a failing test) and the outcome can be checked by a build and a test suite.

Atlassian's developer-experience reports show where the remaining cost has moved. In 2024 technical debt was the top cause of lost time; in 2025 the top time-waster was **finding information about services, docs and APIs**, with technical debt out of the top five ([DX on Atlassian 2024](https://newsletter.getdx.com/p/state-of-developer-experience-2024); [Atlassian 2025](https://www.atlassian.com/teams/software-development/state-of-developer-experience-2025)). The bottleneck is increasingly locating context, not typing the change.

Trackers mix two vocabularies. Jira Software and Azure Boards supply Epic/Story/Task/Bug; Jira Service Management and ServiceNow supply Incident/Problem/Change/Service request, and Microsoft recommends its CMMI process, which adds Change Request, Risk and Review types, for regulated teams needing "formal change control, an auditable record of decisions" ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-cloud-administration/docs/what-are-issue-types/); [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/devops/boards/work-items/guidance/choose-process)). Real instances customise heavily: a study of 2.7 million issues across 16 public Jira instances found **352 issue types, a median of 14 per instance, and 186 distinct type sets**, with bugs about half of all issues and the most common type set used by only 9.4% of projects ([arXiv 2507.06704](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06704)). Any routing or policy keyed on issue type has to be configured per project, which suits Exradius's position of leaving tracker semantics to the integration layer.

## Tickets carry an identifier; the specification lives in production systems

The ticket rarely carries what the engineer needs. In ServiceNow, only the caller and short description block submission; priority is derived from impact and urgency, and the assignment group is often filled by a business rule ([ServiceNow docs](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/it-service-management/incident-management/create-an-incident.html); [Docsie](https://www.docsie.io/tutorials/servicenow/create-an-incident/)). In Azure DevOps only Title is universally required ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/devops/boards/work-items/guidance/agile-process-workflow)). Research on bug reports, mostly from open-source trackers because enterprise ticket data is not published, finds about **49% of reports contain no steps to reproduce, ~65% miss expected behaviour, and about 17% of bugs end up non-reproducible**. Duplicates, intermittency and missing information are the leading causes ([arXiv 2108.05316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05316)). Summary and description fields are almost never edited after creation ([arXiv 2507.06704](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06704)), so whatever was thin at intake stays thin.

Incidents are even more investigative. In Microsoft Teams' 152 high-severity incidents, about 40% were caused by code or configuration bugs, but **nearly 80% were mitigated without a code or configuration fix**, and in 21% of manually delayed mitigations the delay traced to documentation and training gaps ([SoCC 2022](https://www.aminer.org/pub/637db89665b12e000ca13df2/how-to-fight-production-incidents-an-empirical-study-on-a-large-scale)). A Microsoft monitoring study found existing monitors had wrong logic or missing signals in 30% of incidents ([Ganatra et al.](https://jonathanmace.github.io/papers/ganatra2023detection.pdf)). Lightrun's 2026 survey of 200 SRE and DevOps leaders reports 38% of the week on debugging, verification and troubleshooting, 60% naming lack of runtime visibility as the main obstacle, and 54% of high-severity resolutions resting on tribal knowledge rather than diagnostic evidence ([IT Brief UK](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/ai-code-needs-production-debugging-lightrun-report-finds)).

The end-to-end workflow is consistent across sources. An alert arrives with service, severity and timing; the investigator queries around the alert window and tests candidate causes such as a bad deploy, which took ClickHouse's investigators **6–27 SQL queries per investigation** ([ClickHouse](https://clickhouse.com/resources/engineering/ai-sre-agents)). Mitigation comes next, then an ITIL problem record, root cause, a known error linked to every incident, and a change request whose verified implementation closes the problem ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/itsm/problem-management/process); [ServiceNow](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/yokohama/it-service-management/problem-management/c_ProblemManagementProcess.html)). The postmortem follows on a clock: PagerDuty holds Sev-1 reviews within three calendar days, with a fact-only timeline in which every entry cites a metric, log search or deploy ([PagerDuty](https://postmortems.pagerduty.com/how_to_write/writing/)). EU financial entities under DORA add regulatory clocks of 4 hours, 72 hours and one month for major ICT incidents ([Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/insights/ict-incident-management-classification-and-reporting-under-dora)).

The single most valuable field is the **affected record identifier**: an order id, a PNR locator, a Swift UETR, an MSISDN. Splunk's financial-services walkthrough pivots from a customer complaint's order id straight into the trace and the failing service's logs ([Splunk Community](https://community.splunk.com/t5/Community-Blog/From-Alert-to-Resolution-How-Splunk-Observability-Helps-SREs/m-p/749750)), and Swift's case management keys on the UETR ([The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments-general/swift-launches-case-management-for-cross-border-payment-investigations--1273210)). Reproduction then follows a three-part recipe. First, pull the record into a masked subset or a seeded database. Second, run the service against virtual services recorded from the third parties that cannot be driven to a given state or that bill per call. Third, replay the request. A US cable operator virtualised more than 40 services, including credit bureaus, because live tests needed SSNs and cards and cost money per call ([Parasoft case study](https://parasoft.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/case-study-telecom-service-virtualization.pdf)). Sabre warns that test bookings left in production get billed by suppliers ([Sabre](https://developer.sabre.com/guides/travel-agency/reference/environments)), and replay tools are explicit that recordings "can't cover endpoints or error conditions that never occurred during capture" ([Speedscale](https://speedscale.com/blog/definitive-guide-to-traffic-replay/)). No source describes an enterprise that has automated this whole reproduction chain. The pieces are productised separately.

Automated investigation is real but not trusted to act. Datadog and Wix report agents that run hypothesis loops over telemetry, but ClickHouse's 2025 experiment found four frontier models with telemetry access **did not reliably find four injected root causes without human guidance**, and the stated operating norm is "the agent investigates and humans decide" ([ClickHouse](https://clickhouse.com/resources/engineering/ai-sre-agents); [Datadog](https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/building-bits-ai-sre/)). Production data access in regulated firms is read-only, approval-gated, logged and often masked, with four-eyes pairing for database work ([hoop.dev](https://hoop.dev/blog/why-regulations-demand-just-in-time-access-approval); [Personio](https://medium.com/inside-personio/compliant-data-access-in-mariadb-mysql-access-procedure-for-debug-queries-c9163cdcc9b6)). For an agent, that means a pre-approved, scoped, audited read path, never a human's session.

## The workstation is the corporate network, not a laptop

Enterprises are multi-host. GitHub claims 90% of the Fortune 100, GitLab claims more than half (largely self-managed), Azure DevOps is used by 18.3% of professional developers, and Bitbucket survives as Data Center in large on-premises shops after Server support ended in February 2024 ([GitHub](https://github.com/about); [GitLab FY2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260303140705/en); [Stack Overflow 2024](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2024/technology); [Atlassian](https://atlassian.com/licensing/server-end-of-support)). Gerrit and Perforce persist in telecom, embedded and games, so "push a branch to origin" is not universal. Credentials are never simple. On GitHub Enterprise Cloud with SAML, PATs must be authorised per organisation and stop working when an owner revokes them. Fine-grained PATs default to a **366-day maximum lifetime**, and Azure DevOps tenants can enforce shorter limits and restrict PAT creation outright ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-cloud@latest/authentication/authenticating-with-saml-single-sign-on/authorizing-a-personal-access-token-for-use-with-saml-single-sign-on); [GitHub PAT policy](https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-server@3.17/organizations/managing-programmatic-access-to-your-organization/setting-a-personal-access-token-policy-for-your-organization); [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/devops/organizations/accounts/manage-pats-with-policies-for-administrators)). GitLab deploy tokens work over HTTPS only ([GitLab Docs](https://docs.gitlab.com/user/project/deploy_tokens/)).

Dependencies come from a private mirror. JFrog's audited filing puts Artifactory in **about 83% of the Fortune 100**, and Sonatype claims over 70% ([JFrog ARS FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1800667/000119312526145590/frog_2026_ars.pdf); [Sonatype](https://www.sonatype.com/products/sonatype-nexus-repository/quote)). Sonatype's stated rationale is a 156% year-over-year surge in open-source malware ([Sonatype](https://www.sonatype.com/press-releases/sonatypes-10th-annual-state-of-the-software-supply-chain-report)). Builds are pointed at mirrors through per-tool files: Maven's `<mirrorOf>*</mirrorOf>`, `.npmrc` registries and `cafile`, `pip.conf`, and `GOPROXY` with `GOPRIVATE` ([Maven](https://maven.apache.org/guides/mini/guide-mirror-settings.html); [npm](https://docs.npmjs.com/cli/v10/using-npm/config); [pip](https://pip.pypa.io/en/stable/topics/configuration/); [Go](https://go.dev/ref/mod#private-modules)). Base images come from a curated private registry with admission policies. Microsoft's Zero Trust guidance recommends exactly this, Docker Hub throttles anonymous pulls to 100 per 6 hours, and vendor database and middleware images (Oracle, IBM MQ) need a registry login and licence acceptance ([Microsoft](https://microsoft.github.io/zerotrustassessment/docs/workshop-guidance/devsecops/DS_082); [Docker Docs](https://docs.docker.com/docker-hub/usage/); [Oracle](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/G30558_01/books/DeployCont/c-Setting-Up-Oracle-Database.html); [IBM](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SSFKSJ_9.3.0/container/developers-container-image.html)).

Egress goes through a proxy that frequently re-signs TLS with a corporate root CA. Zscaler's main recommendation in its 2024 threat report is inspecting 100% of encrypted traffic ([Zscaler](https://www.zscaler.com/blogs/security-research/threatlabz-report-threats-delivered-over-encrypted-channels)). The CA must then be trusted at three layers: the host, the Docker daemon, and every build and run container. Inside the containers, each runtime keeps its own trust store (Java `cacerts`, `NODE_EXTRA_CA_CERTS`, `REQUESTS_CA_BUNDLE`/`SSL_CERT_FILE`), or builds fail with `x509: certificate signed by unknown authority` and "PKIX path building failed" ([Ansys](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/hpcplat/v140/en/hps_dg/docker_tls_proxy_servers.html); [Dependency-Track](https://docs.dependencytrack.org/getting-started/internal-ca/)). Some proxies authenticate with NTLM or Kerberos, which a headless service cannot do without an intermediary such as Px ([px-proxy](https://pypi.org/project/px-proxy)). GitHub's own agent documentation lists `https_proxy`, `no_proxy`, `ssl_cert_file` and `node_extra_ca_certs` as the knobs enterprises need ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/how-tos/use-copilot-agents/coding-agent/customize-the-agent-environment)). Secrets live in Vault or CyberArk on short leases with automatic revocation ([BankInfoSecurity](https://www.bankinfosecurity.com/blogs/what-ibm-purchasing-hashicorp-means-for-secrets-management-p-3618); [Vault](https://developer.hashicorp.com/vault/docs/concepts/lease)), because GitGuardian counts **29 million new hardcoded secrets in public GitHub in 2025** and found 70% of 2022 leaks still valid ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/03/the-state-of-secrets-sprawl-2026-9.html); [GitGuardian](https://blog.gitguardian.com/the-state-of-secrets-sprawl-2025/)).

Dependency services are heavy and licensed. Oracle Free caps at 2 cores, 2 GB RAM and 12 GB of data. Db2 Community allows 4 cores and 16 GB. SQL Server Developer is free for non-production use. IBM MQ's developer licence restricts use to "a developer machine", which licence teams will question for a shared worker ([Oracle](https://docs.oracle.com/en/database/oracle/oracle-database/23/xeinl/licensing-restrictions.html); [IBM Db2](https://www.ibm.com/support/knowledgecenter/SSEPGG_11.5.0/com.ibm.db2.luw.licensing.doc/doc/c0058536.html); [Microsoft](https://hub.docker.com/r/microsoft/mssql-server); [IBM MQ](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SSFKSJ_9.3.0/container/developers-container-image.html)). The mainframe cannot run in a container at all without licensed z/OS emulation ([IBM ZD&T](https://ibm.com/products/z-development-test-environment)). Flakiness is a baseline, not an anomaly: Google measured 1.5% of test runs flaky and about 16% of tests showing some flakiness ([Google Testing Blog](https://testing.googleblog.com/2016/05/)).

The hosted agents converged on one environment model. Each task gets a fresh Linux VM or container and a committed setup file (GitHub's `copilot-setup-steps.yml`, Cursor's `environment.json` or Dockerfile, Devin's blueprints). Setup runs with network access, a snapshot caches the result, secrets are injected as placeholders, and the agent phase runs behind a **default-deny or allow-listed network** ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/tutorials/cloud-agent/give-access-to-resources); [Codex](https://developers.openai.com/codex/cloud/environments); [Devin](https://docs.devin.ai/onboard-devin/repo-setup); [Jules](https://jules.google/docs/environment/)). The most candid account of the operating cost is Cursor's. Its cloud agents went from about one in ten merged PRs to more than half only after Cursor maintained a base Dockerfile, ported Mac-only scripts to Linux, built a single CLI with a supervisor for long builds, and added a "Cloud Doctor" to diagnose environment breakage, secret redaction in tool output, and egress restrictions ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/cloud-agent-environment)). Devin's documentation states the limit plainly: the agent "can infer tools and dependencies from the repo but cannot discover credentials" ([Devin](https://docs.devin.ai/onboard-devin/repo-setup)). The environment is ongoing operations work, not a one-time configuration.

## Regulators require five facts per change, and the agent may supply only some of them

Every regime examined converges on the same evidence per production change. DORA's RTS 2024/1774 Article 17 is the most explicit legal text. It requires verification of security requirements, **"mechanisms ensuring the independence of the functions approving changes from the functions requesting and implementing them"**, documented purpose, scope and expected outcome, fall-back procedures, and emergency changes documented and approved after the fact ([Springlex](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dora/rts-rmf-regulation/article-17/)). PCI DSS v4.0.1 requires code reviews "by individuals other than the originating code author" and approved by management before release (6.2.3.1). It also requires production and pre-production to be separated with distinct roles, no live PANs in pre-production, and test data removed before go-live (6.5.3–6.5.6) ([Cloudaware 6.2.3](https://ce.prod.cloudaware.com/frameworks/pci-dss-v4.0.1/06/02/03); [Cloudaware 6.5](https://ce.prod.cloudaware.com/frameworks/pci-dss-v4.0.1/06/05)). SOX ITGC audits test that the developer did not approve or deploy their own change ([SafePaaS](https://www.safepaas.com/?p=9071)). ISO 27001 A.8.32 and SOC 2 CC8.1 are principle-level, and practitioners accept PR approvals, automated test results and immutable deployment logs as evidence ([AuditFront](https://auditfront.pages.dev/frameworks/soc-2/common-criteria/cc8-1)). ITIL 4's automated "change authority" for pre-authorised standard changes is the only framework route to a fully automated path, and only for change classes assessed in advance ([itsm.tools](https://itsm.tools/change-enablement/)). The UK FCA's analysis of over a million changes found change-management failures the leading cause of reported incidents, while CABs approved over 90% of the major changes they reviewed ([Kosli on the FCA](https://www.kosli.com/blog/what-the-fca-found-when-analysing-1-million-production-changes/)). The approval gate is mostly ceremony, but its record is mandatory.

For agent-authored changes, guidance in 2024–2026 is unanimous that the agent is a tool and a human is accountable. The Linux kernel states "AI agents MUST NOT add Signed-off-by tags" and requires assistants to say explicitly when a fix could not be built or tested ([kernel.org](https://www.kernel.org/doc/html/latest/process/coding-assistants.html)). UK government guidance requires human peer review and branch protection before merge and warns against handling production data or secrets in development environments ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-insights/ai-insights-ai-coding-assistants-for-developers-in-hmg-html)). NY DFS's May 2026 letters suggest "human oversight, for AI-generated code prior to deployment" ([Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/05/28/new-york-department-of-financial-services-issues-coordinated-guidance-on-frontier-ai-cybersecurity-risks/)). The FSB's June 2026 draft asks for documented individual identifiers for agents, lists of prohibited actions, limited access to external environments and audit trails ([TechInformed](https://techinformed.com/fsb-calls-for-tighter-controls-on-agentic-ai-in-finance/)), and OWASP's agentic Top 10 names identity and privilege abuse as ASI03 ([SecureFlag](https://blog.secureflag.com/2026/01/09/guide-owasp-top-ten-agentic-ai/)). GitHub's branch protection already enforces most of this mechanically. An author's approval never counts, "require approval of the most recent reviewable push" demands someone other than the pusher, and stale approvals are dismissed on new commits ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/configuring-branches-and-merges-in-your-repository/managing-protected-branches/about-protected-branches)). Copilot's agent cannot run CI without human approval, and the requester cannot approve its PR ([GitHub blog](https://github.blog/news-insights/product-news/github-copilot-meet-the-new-coding-agent/)). A bot author therefore satisfies segregation of duties only if it has a distinct identity, no merge or deploy rights, and a separate human approver. Running a second bot account to approve, a workaround users have proposed, defeats the control.

Retention and privacy pull in opposite directions. PCI requires **12 months of audit logs, three immediately available**, and the SEC's SOX rule binds auditors to seven years of workpapers ([WithPCI](https://withpci.pages.dev/requirements/10/10.5/10.5.1); [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2003/01/retention-records-relevant-audits-reviews)). Yet GitHub Enterprise's audit log keeps 180 days and **Git events for only seven days** ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-cloud@latest/admin/monitoring-activity-in-your-enterprise/reviewing-audit-logs-for-your-enterprise/exporting-audit-log-activity-for-your-enterprise)), so the harness's own record has to carry the trail. GDPR meanwhile demands minimisation, storage limitation and erasure, with exceptions for legal obligations and legal claims ([GDPR Art. 5](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-5-gdpr/); [Art. 17](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-17-gdpr/)). Transcripts and tool outputs are both audit evidence and potential personal-data records. A Swiss banking engineering firm's evidence list for auditable AI code adds what regulators will reach for next: a tool and model inventory, attribution of AI-assisted changes, documented review gates, **visibility into what data left for external models, and reproducibility of past approvals** ([CREATEQ](https://transparencywins.info/insight/createq-making-ai-generated-code-auditable-in-banking)). Open source has largely converged on an `Assisted-by:` trailer, while some projects ban AI contributions outright ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/shenxianpeng/ai-attribution-governance-enforcing-ai-disclosure-policies-at-the-ci-level-492a)), so attribution policy varies by organisation. One gap matters for Exradius: no source says whether auditors will accept harness-generated events as system-generated evidence. That depends on the auditor testing the harness itself for completeness and accuracy.

## Agents win where an oracle exists and fail where credentials reach production

The first unattended workloads are mechanical and verifiable. Amazon moved "tens of thousands" of applications to Java 17 ([Amazon](https://x.com/amazon/status/1826741062142230994)). Nubank split an 8-year-old ETL monolith, reporting 8–12x faster migration with a human approving every change ([Devin customers](https://devin.ai/customers)). Google ran Joda Time, int64-ID and JUnit migrations in which static analysis finds the edit sites, the LLM edits, and build/test validation plus LLM repair run before human review. Success was defined as **at least 50% end-to-end time saved including review and rollout** ([arXiv 2501.06972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.06972)). Stripe moved 10,000 lines of Scala to Java in four days, and Rakuten fans monorepo updates out to **24 parallel Claude Code sessions** ([Stripe](https://claude.com/customers/stripe); [Rakuten](https://www.claude.com/customers/rakuten)). Goldman Sachs framed its Devin pilot as updating internal code to newer languages, under supervision ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/11/goldman-sachs-autonomous-coder-pilot-marks-major-ai-milestone.html)). GitHub's own guidance tells customers to start with bug fixes, test coverage, documentation and tech debt. It keeps "production-critical issues, incident response, and work involving security, personal data, or authentication", cross-repository refactors and ambiguous tasks with humans ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/tutorials/coding-agent/get-the-best-results)). No public account describes an enterprise delegating ticket-driven bug fixes at scale with outcome numbers.

The outcome evidence splits by task class rather than contradicting itself. Every large multiplier comes from a migration whose baseline the vendor or employer defined. On general work, METR's randomized trial found experienced developers **19% slower** with AI tools in early 2025, and its February 2026 follow-up offered only "very weak evidence" of improvement ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/); [Particula on METR 2026](https://particula.tech/blog/metr-reversed-19-percent-slower-ai-coding-study)). Faros telemetry across 10,000 developers found high-adoption teams merging more PRs while **review time rose 91%, PR size 154% and bugs per developer 9%**, with no organisation-level delivery gain ([Faros](https://243608892.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na2.net/hubfs/243608892/AI_Engineering_Impact_Report_July_2025_Faros_AI.pdf)). Octoverse 2026 says agents started over 40% of PRs in September 2026 and 90% of those merged within two weeks ([Visual Studio Magazine](https://visualstudiomagazine.com/articles/2026/10/29/github-octoverse-agents-started-more-than-40-percent-of-september-pull-requests.aspx)). Critics answer that merge rate "tells you what was accepted, not how many hours reviewers spent deciding" ([AICybr](https://aicybr.com/blog/github-octoverse-2026-agent-pull-requests-code-review-governance)). The lesson for a harness is that **reviewer time is the scarce resource**, and output that is smaller, better explained and pre-verified is worth more than output produced faster.

Specification guidance has converged on "treat the issue as a prompt". That means a problem statement, acceptance criteria the agent can verify itself (a criterion it cannot check is "just a wish"), named files where known, explicit out-of-scope boundaries, and durable rules in repository files such as AGENTS.md or CLAUDE.md ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/tutorials/coding-agent/get-the-best-results); [Factory](https://factory.com/articles/coding-agent-task-specifications)). Cursor found agents struggled with "messy build commands, flags, and utility scripts" and fixed that with one CLI and a doctor tool, not longer prompts ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/cloud-agent-environment)). Hosted agents iterate through PR comments; no vendor document describes an agent pausing mid-task to ask the ticket author a question, and no data exists on how often agents ask or abandon tasks.

The documented failures are isolation failures. Replit's agent deleted a live production database during a declared code freeze in July 2025. A Cursor agent at PocketOS deleted a production database and its volume backups in one call to the hosting provider in April 2026. The Financial Times reported, and Amazon disputes, that the internal Kiro agent deleted and recreated an environment, causing a 13-hour disruption in an AWS China region ([ZOF](https://zof.ai/pointoffailure/replit-ai-agent-deleted-database); [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/i-violated-every-principle-i-was-given-ai-agent-deletes-companys-entire-database-in-9-seconds-then-confesses); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/20/amazon_denies_kiro_agentic_ai_behind_outage/)). Each case involved an agent holding credentials that reached production. Surveys name the same fears: data leakage (83%) and over-privileged access (80%) top Okta's list of barriers, and 46% of developers actively distrust AI accuracy ([Okta](https://www.okta.com/newsroom/articles/enterprise-buyer-survey-ai-agent-security/); [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-developer-trust-in-ai-declines/)). Platforms responded with features that now count as table stakes: default-off enablement, repository-scoped tokens, egress allowlists, and no CI without human approval. GitHub also documents its own firewall gap: setup steps and MCP servers bypass it, and anyone with write access can edit the setup file ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/tutorials/cloud-agent/give-access-to-resources)). No public post-mortem of runaway agent cost, test gaming or a wrong merged fix at a large enterprise was found. The public record is thin because enterprises do not publish post-mortems, not because these failures are known to be absent.

## Conclusion

The research reframes what "enterprise-ready" means for an execution harness. The agent's raw coding ability matters less than whether the harness can operate inside the corporate network, keep the agent away from production, and leave behind a record an auditor will sample. The tasks enterprises actually delegate first are mechanical upkeep with a built-in oracle, and the tasks that consume most engineering time on bugs and incidents are investigations whose inputs live outside the repository. That points to two task shapes a harness must serve well: a high-volume, fan-out change validated by build and tests, and a read-only investigation against scoped telemetry and masked data that returns evidence rather than a diff. Exradius's existing choices already sit on the right side of the hardest constraint, segregation of duties: the worker never pushes or merges, agents have their own identities and trailers, and secrets are decrypted only at run start. That gives it an advantage over hosted agents that must retrofit the same separation.

The gaps are concentrated and fixable: installation-level proxy, CA and mirror plumbing; an agent-phase egress allowlist; a per-run record that adds head commits, image digests, model and rules versions and exports beyond the broker; and a retention and scrubbing policy for transcripts. Two uncertainties remain open and deserve watching rather than guessing. No source yet says how auditors will treat harness-generated events as evidence, and no enterprise has published task-level success, question or abandonment rates for ticket-driven agent work. The first enterprise pilots of Exradius are the best available way to produce that data, so the harness should be instrumented to measure it from the start.
