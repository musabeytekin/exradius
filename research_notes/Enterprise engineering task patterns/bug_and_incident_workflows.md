# Bug and production-incident workflows in large regulated enterprises (intake → investigation → reproduction → fix → verification → closure)

Scope note for the writer: I prioritised primary documentation (Google SRE book, PagerDuty's open postmortem guide, ServiceNow docs, Sabre developer docs, Swift, ESMA/DORA, peer-reviewed reproduction studies) and clearly-labelled vendor material (Parasoft, Speedscale, Datadog, ClickHouse, Lightrun, Tonic/K2view, hoop.dev). I found no first-party engineering blog from a named airline, bank or telecom describing its defect-reproduction practice end to end; where I cite vendors or case studies for industry practice I say so. Several pages could not be fetched (Atlassian postmortem handbook/template, Gartner TDM market page, Personio Medium post, one Sabre page) and their content is taken from search summaries and marked as such.

## Q1. End-to-end workflow: who is involved, which systems and artifacts at each step

### Takeaway
Across the sources the workflow is consistently: alert or report → incident record (ServiceNow/PagerDuty/Jira) with service, severity, timing and an affected-record identifier → on-call/service team correlates metrics, traces and logs around the alert window and the specific record → mitigation (rollback, flag, workaround) → problem record and root-cause work → fix behind change control → postmortem/RCA with linked tickets, commits and changes. The "investigate" step is the one every automation vendor targets; the "judge and approve" steps stay human.

### Cited Findings
- An alert arrives "with service, severity, and timing metadata"; the investigator then "runs queries around the alert window", drafts candidate causes ("a bad deploy or saturated dependency") and runs targeted queries to test each; ClickHouse measured 6–27 SQL queries per investigation (ClickHouse, updated July 2026) — [ClickHouse, AI SRE agents](https://clickhouse.com/resources/engineering/ai-sre-agents)
- The operating norm is "the agent investigates and humans decide": remediation approval, blast-radius judgement, business trade-offs, SLOs, architecture review and postmortems stay with people; "propose-and-approve is the production norm", autonomous execution is limited to "well-understood, low-blast-radius runbook actions" — [ClickHouse](https://clickhouse.com/resources/engineering/ai-sre-agents)
- Splunk's financial-services walkthrough: a customer complaint carrying an order ID is turned into a trace search ("filter directly to the problematic order ID that triggered the initial complaint") and from the trace into the failing service's logs and metrics — [Splunk Community, From Alert to Resolution](https://community.splunk.com/t5/Community-Blog/From-Alert-to-Resolution-How-Splunk-Observability-Helps-SREs/m-p/749750)
- ITIL 4 flow: incident restored on the incident record; a problem record is opened/linked when the fault persists; a problem with documented root cause and workaround becomes a known error; the known error is linked to all related incidents and to the request for change; only the implemented and verified change closes the problem — [Atlassian, Problem management process](https://www.atlassian.com/itsm/problem-management/process); [IT Process Maps, Checklist Problem Record](https://wiki.en.it-processmaps.com/index.php/Checklist_Problem_Record); [ServiceNow, Problem Management process](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/yokohama/it-service-management/problem-management/c_ProblemManagementProcess.html)
- ITIL 4 replaced the single CAB with a "change authority" that can be "a person, a team such as a change advisory board (CAB), or an automated rule, depending on the risk"; emergency changes use an expedited ECAB path and are reviewed afterwards; the post-implementation review records the change as successful, failed or partially successful — [itsm.tools, Change Enablement in ITIL 4](https://itsm.tools/change-enablement/); [InvGate, Emergency change process](https://blog.invgate.com/emergency-change-control-process)
- PagerDuty's postmortem process: the Incident Commander names one responder as postmortem owner; the owner schedules the meeting, builds the timeline, gathers input, opens follow-up tickets, drafts external messaging and shares results; status values Draft → In Review → Reviewed → Closed — [PagerDuty Postmortem Guide, Writing](https://postmortems.pagerduty.com/how_to_write/writing/)
- Regulated-firm overlay (EU financial entities under DORA): initial notification 4 hours from classifying an ICT incident as major (no later than 24 hours from awareness), intermediate report within 72 hours of the initial notification, final report one month after the (latest) intermediate report; ESMA expects at least one intermediate report per month while open; from November 2027 all Swift investigation requests/responses must be ISO 20022 camt.110/111 via Case Management — [Fieldfisher, ICT incident management under DORA](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/insights/ict-incident-management-classification-and-reporting-under-dora); [ESMA, DORA incident reporting operational instructions (PDF)](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-09/DORA_Incident_reporting_-_Operational_instructions.pdf); [FD Capital on RTS 2025/301 timelines](https://www.fdcapital.co.uk/ict-incident-reporting-dora-timelines-templates/); [Swift, ISO 20022 chapter 6](https://www.swift.com/standards/iso-20022/supercharge-your-payments-business/chapter-6)

### Inferences
- Two record systems always coexist in these firms: the operational one (PagerDuty/Opsgenie alert, Slack war room) and the ITSM/regulatory one (ServiceNow incident → problem → change; DORA reports). Any automated investigator's output must be attachable to the ITSM record, not just the chat.
- The DORA clock (4h/72h/1 month) and the Sev-1 postmortem clock (PagerDuty: meeting within 3 calendar days) mean "assemble the timeline and the evidence" is time-boxed work that recurs on every major incident — a natural automation target.

### Gaps
- No primary source from a named airline/bank/telecom laying out its own incident-to-fix RACI. ITIL-practice sources are vendor/training blogs, not the AXELOS text.
- Atlassian's incident handbook and postmortem template pages returned only navigation (truncated/403), so Atlassian's severity thresholds and template fields are not cited here.

## Q2. Intake: what a report carries, how often it is incomplete

### Takeaway
Ticket systems make only a title and caller mandatory; impact/urgency/CI/assignment are configured per instance and often auto-derived. Academic studies consistently find a large share of bug reports lack steps to reproduce or expected behaviour, and roughly 13–17% of reports end up non-reproducible, with duplicates, intermittency and missing information the top causes.

### Cited Findings
- ServiceNow incident form: "Short description" (brief summary), "Urgency" ("how long the resolution can be delayed"), "Impact" tied to the affected business service, "Priority ... based on impact and urgency" (derived, not entered), "Configuration item that is affected", "Assignment group" ("Group who will work on the incident"), which a business rule can populate from the CI or service offering — [ServiceNow docs, Create an incident](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/it-service-management/incident-management/create-an-incident.html); third-party tutorial: only Caller and Short description block submission when empty — [Docsie ServiceNow tutorial](https://www.docsie.io/tutorials/servicenow/create-an-incident/)
- Developers expect three components in a bug report: Observed Behaviour, Expected Behaviour, Steps to Reproduce (Bettenburg et al., cited in Rahman et al.); Chaparro et al. found ~65% of reports miss expected behaviour and 49% contain no steps to reproduce; Joorabchi et al. (2014) found about 17% of reported bugs are non-reproducible, and non-reproducible reports can delay fixes by e.g. three months (all as cited in Rahman, Khomh, Castelluccio, ICSME 2020) — [arXiv 2108.05316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05316)
- Rahman et al. (ICSME 2020; 576 non-reproducible reports from Firefox and Eclipse; 13 professional developers): 11 factors; duplication ~29% (27–31% per system), intermittency 14% on average (up to 26% of Firefox bugs), missing information ≥8% (≈14% in Eclipse), ambiguous/outdated specification ≈8%, third-party defects/memory misuse/restricted security access together ≈12%, miscellaneous ≈11%; 75% of non-reproducible bugs have a single cause, 22% two, 2% three; 70–90% of developers agreed with the factors — [arXiv 2108.05316 (PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.05316)
- How developers cope (same study): duplicates closed WORKSFORME/DUPLICATE; false positives closed INVALID with an explanation; for intermittent bugs they ask for crash dumps, debugging/system logs, delegate to colleagues with experience and close if not seen again for e.g. 12 weeks; for missing information they request steps/screencast and close WONTFIX after e.g. two weeks; for performance bugs they request profiles; the authors recommend "sandbox for bug reproduction" tools — [arXiv 2108.05316 (PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.05316)
- Android reproduction study (Johnson et al., SANER 2022; 180 reproducible GitHub bug reports): bugs are reported multi-modally, the environment needed for reproduction is not always provided, steps often lack specificity or completeness — [arXiv 2301.01235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01235); a follow-on NLP/RL reproduction paper reports 92.2% of its Android reports had at least one missing step, equating to 43.2% unmapped GUI actions (figure from search summary of the paper, not verified against full text) — [arXiv 2301.07775](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.07775)
- Payments intake specifics: Swift's case management keys on the UETR, which "provides visibility into a payment's status and location at every stage"; an Australian NPP change request notes the current ISO 20022 model "restricts each investigation case (camt.110 / camt.111) to a single underlying transaction reference" and asks for multiple references per case to cover batch incidents — [The Paypers on Swift Case Management](https://thepaypers.com/payments-general/swift-launches-case-management-for-cross-border-payment-investigations--1273210); [ISO 20022 change request](https://www.iso20022.org/milestone/25064/download)
- Airline intake specifics: Sabre's test environment exists to "send and cancel test PNRs"; test bookings made in production must be cancelled or "you and possibly your customers will be billed by suppliers"; certification-environment data is "limited and subject to availability" for cache-based APIs and "not all airline carrier data (e.g. ticketless carriers)" is returned there (quotes via search summary of Sabre developer docs) — [Sabre developer docs, Environments](https://developer.sabre.com/guides/travel-agency/reference/environments); [Sabre, Testing](https://developer.sabre.com/testing)

### Inferences
- In enterprise intake the "record identifier" (PNR locator, order id, UETR/transaction id, MSISDN/account) is the single most valuable field because it is the key that both the trace search and the database lookup pivot on; the Splunk walkthrough and Swift case management both treat it that way.
- The open-source numbers (49% no steps, ~17% non-reproducible) are the best available proxies; enterprise ticket data is not published. The 12.77–24.26% non-reproducible range attributed to a Bugzilla review in search results could not be verified and is omitted from findings.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on incompleteness of enterprise ServiceNow/Jira incident records (fields missing, time to triage) — only open-source bug-tracker studies.
- PagerDuty/Opsgenie incident field schemas were not fetched; the DORA major-incident template fields (beyond timelines) are in the RTS/ITS and not extracted here.

## Q3. Investigation: correlating logs, traces and metrics; reading production data with restricted access; time spent

### Takeaway
Investigation pivots on a correlation key (trace id, order id, UETR) through the observability stack; production data access in regulated firms is read-only, approval-gated, time-boxed, logged and often masked. Debugging/troubleshooting consumes roughly a third of engineering time in recent surveys, and lack of runtime visibility is the most-cited blocker.

### Cited Findings
- Splunk example: error spike → trace filtered to the complaining customer's order ID → root service → logs — [Splunk Community](https://community.splunk.com/t5/Community-Blog/From-Alert-to-Resolution-How-Splunk-Observability-Helps-SREs/m-p/749750)
- Data an investigator needs (ClickHouse): logs, metrics, traces (error rates, latency percentiles, log patterns, trace exemplars); recent deployments and config changes correlated with alert timing; runbooks and incident history for known patterns; access via APIs or an MCP server with "read-only credentials scoped to observability tables"; constraints: short retention limits historical comparison, dropped high-cardinality fields limit correlation, slow or per-query-billed stores break multi-query loops — [ClickHouse](https://clickhouse.com/resources/engineering/ai-sre-agents)
- Datadog Bits Investigation (Jan 2026) proposes root causes, checks each with targeted telemetry queries, keeps or discards, decomposes surviving hypotheses into sub-hypotheses; outputs confirmed findings, supporting log evidence, summary and rejected alternatives — [Datadog, Building Bits AI SRE](https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/building-bits-ai-sre/)
- Lightrun "State of AI-Powered Engineering" 2026 (200 SRE/DevOps leaders at large companies, with Global Surveyz): developers spend 38% of their week on debugging, verification and troubleshooting; 60% name lack of runtime visibility as the main obstacle to resolving incidents; in 54% of high-severity resolutions teams relied on tribal knowledge rather than diagnostic evidence; 44% say failed AI-SRE/APM investigations stem from missing execution-level data (variable state, memory, request flow); 77% lack confidence in observability for automated RCA — [IT Brief UK on Lightrun report, 14 Apr 2026](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/ai-code-needs-production-debugging-lightrun-report-finds)
- Older baseline (ClusterHQ 2016, 386 respondents): 43% of app developers spend 10–25% of their time debugging errors found in production — [BetaNews 2016](https://betanews.com/article/developers-debugging-production-errors)
- APMdigest/troubleshooting survey (~336 respondents, undated): troubleshooting app issues is the top activity, chosen as a top-3 task by 48% — [APMdigest](https://apmdigest.com/node/9637)
- Production DB access procedure (Personio, HR SaaS — not a bank; content from search summary, Medium returned 403): engineers must pair for database work ("four eyes principle"), every action auditable, approvals via Slack, logs to CloudWatch; replaced a slower ticket-based process — [Inside Personio, Compliant data access](https://medium.com/inside-personio/compliant-data-access-in-mariadb-mysql-access-procedure-for-debug-queries-c9163cdcc9b6)
- Vendor framing of the regulated pattern (hoop.dev): production "locked down by default", access only "for planned tasks or emergencies after explicit approval"; PCI DSS requires all production access logged so auditors see "who accessed what and when"; "masked data snapshots" are a frozen DB copy with names/emails/addresses transformed — [hoop.dev, JIT and regulations](https://hoop.dev/blog/why-regulations-demand-just-in-time-access-approval); [hoop.dev, masked snapshots](https://hoop.dev/blog/masked-data-snapshots-with-secure-database-access-gateways)
- Payments: a 2025 arXiv paper computes processing times between consecutive ISO 20022 message exchanges in an instant-payment system and applies anomaly detection to localise failures and classify incidents — [arXiv 2510.21710](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21710v1)
- Telecom billing: catching a billing defect "requires following a usage record end to end, from the network event through mediation and rating to the invoice line"; suites passing on sample data can fail on month-end runs at production volume; flat-file CDR analysis is used to troubleshoot customer complaints and find duplicate billing (vendor sources) — [ContextQA telecom testing](https://contextqa.com/industries/telecom-testing/); [Gigasheet CDR analysis](https://gigasheet.com/post/analyze-telecom-call-detail-record-cdr-flat-files)

### Inferences
- The investigator's minimal toolset is: a trace/log search keyed by correlation id over the alert window, deployment/config change history, and a read-only path to the affected record(s). Everything else (runbooks, past incidents) raises precision.
- Four-eyes/JIT access is a human-latency step; for an agent it translates into a pre-approved read-only, masked or replica credential plus an audit log, or an approval hop for each query.

### Gaps
- No published measurement of how long production-data access requests take in banks/telecoms, nor of median investigation duration per incident class.
- No bank/airline/telecom first-party description of their read-replica/masked-view practice; evidence is a non-bank SaaS post (via search) and vendors.

## Q4. Reproduction when the data lives in production: TDM, masking/subsetting/synthetic data, service virtualisation, seeded DBs, replay

### Takeaway
Enterprises reproduce with a masked/subsetted or synthetic copy of the relevant records (TDM), virtual services recorded from real third parties (Parasoft/Broadcom/WireMock) and, increasingly, recorded-traffic replay with dependency mocks (Speedscale/GoReplay). Each approach has a known blind spot: recordings only contain what happened during capture, sandboxes lack production data parity, and masked data can break edge cases.

### Cited Findings
- Gartner's TDM market definition: modern TDM "leverage[s] synthetic data generation, alongside data subsetting and masking techniques, to provide realistic yet secure test data" (quote via search summary; page returned 403) — [Gartner Peer Insights, Test Data Management](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/market/test-data-management)
- Vendor positioning (May 2024 Tonic blog averaging G2 + Gartner Peer Insights): Tonic 4.5, K2view 4.4, Delphix 4.05; Tonic claims patented subsetting that preserves referential integrity; K2view models by business entity (one customer's data across systems); Delphix virtualises full databases from a staging copy and masks after ingestion — [Tonic.ai, TDM software](https://www.tonic.ai/blog/test-data-management-software); [K2view, Delphix vs K2view](https://www.k2view.com/blog/delphix-vs-k2view); [Perforce/Delphix comparison](https://www.perforce.com/blog/pdx/delphix-vs-k2view)
- Telecom service virtualisation case (Parasoft case study, PDF dated Dec 2024; US cable/broadband operator, 32M customers, 41 states, 100,000+ staff): motivations were third-party services (Experian, ID Analytics) that did not scale and cost money per call, limited lab availability, and "complex and sensitive test data, like Social Security numbers, credit cards, names, phones, and email" making live-environment testing "extremely complex"; approach: "virtual proxies to record and play back test data from live services", toggling live/virtual, hybrid response modes "combining virtual and real services based on matching conditions" to test "specific credit scores or past debts"; results: 40+ services virtualised, >20% more mobile-app test coverage, tens of thousands of dollars/year saved in third-party test costs, $7,600/year saved on devices/lab — [Parasoft telecom case study (PDF)](https://parasoft.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/case-study-telecom-service-virtualization.pdf)
- Parasoft claims a 74% decrease in critical defect escape ratio and support for 120+ message types/protocols; it argues WireMock lacks protocol breadth, low-code modelling and centralized environment orchestration (competitor's view) — [Parasoft Virtualize](https://www.parasoft.com/products/parasoft-virtualize/); Broadcom Service Virtualization is positioned for "complex legacy, mainframe, and middleware environments" — [FitGap, Broadcom SV](https://us.fitgap.com/products/010795/broadcom-service-virtualization)
- Traffic replay (Speedscale guide, Mar 2026, updated Nov 2026): proxymock records inbound requests and outbound dependency calls (HTTP, gRPC, Postgres, MySQL, Redis), `replay` resends saved inbound requests to the app under test, `mock` serves recorded dependency responses and `--no-passthrough` rejects unmatched calls; sensitive data must be reviewed in bodies/headers/query strings and masked, secrets removed, credentials replaced; transforms handle dynamic values (tokens, order-ID correlation); limitations: recordings "can't cover endpoints or error conditions that never occurred during capture", recorded responses go stale and "may omit error cases", database state can cause unrelated failures; CI use needs Pro/Enterprise — [Speedscale, Definitive guide to traffic replay](https://speedscale.com/blog/definitive-guide-to-traffic-replay/)
- GoReplay (LGPL, single Go binary): "capture the exact requests that caused an error and replay them in a development environment"; middleware for filtering/rewriting — [helpmetest, GoReplay](https://helpmetest.com/blog/goreplay-record-replay-production-traffic/)
- Airline: Sabre's CERT environment for test PNRs has limited, availability-dependent data and omits some carriers; production test bookings must be cancelled and test tickets should not be issued in production — [Sabre, Environments](https://developer.sabre.com/guides/travel-agency/reference/environments)
- Telecom: test-call generators compare known test calls against the daily CDR feed to find faulty switches; realistic volume is "mandatory rather than optional" for billing defects — [ContextQA](https://contextqa.com/industries/telecom-testing/); [Scribd, Test Call Generators](https://www.scribd.com/document/464097830/Test-Call-Generators)
- Rahman et al. recommend sandbox tooling for reproduction; developers cope with intermittent bugs by requesting crash dumps and logs rather than by reproducing — [arXiv 2108.05316 (PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.05316)

### Inferences
- The practical reproduction recipe in these firms is: (1) pull the affected record(s) by identifier into a masked subset (TDM) or a seeded local DB; (2) stand the service up against virtual services/mocks recorded from the real third party (GDS, credit bureau, payment rail, network element); (3) replay the request(s) that carried the correlation id. Where (1) is impossible, engineers fall back to logs/dumps and cannot reproduce — the 14% "intermittent" bucket.
- External systems that bill per call or cannot be driven to a specific state (Sabre, Experian, Swift) are exactly why service virtualisation is standard in airline/bank/telecom test estates.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) study measuring how often production defects are reproducible in masked/subsetted data versus full production copies.
- No Amadeus-specific sandbox/data-parity documentation found; Broadcom SV mainframe specifics not fetched. Informatica TDM and Gretel were not covered by any fetched source.
- Delphix "refresh to point in time" and K2view entity-based extraction for a single customer are vendor claims without an enterprise case study in the sources fetched.

## Q5. Verification: local/ephemeral runs, integration/UAT, canary and flags, proof before change approval

### Takeaway
Fixes are proven by reproducing in a non-production environment, then rolled out behind canaries/feature flags with kill switches; in regulated firms flag changes themselves go through approval and audit. Survey data suggests confirmation in production still takes several redeploy cycles.

### Cited Findings
- PagerDuty analysis guidance: "reproduce the issue in a non-production environment where possible" as part of postmortem analysis — [PagerDuty, Writing](https://postmortems.pagerduty.com/how_to_write/writing/)
- Lightrun 2026: 43% of AI-generated code that passed QA/staging still needed manual production debugging; 88% say two to three manual redeploy cycles are needed to confirm an AI-suggested fix works in production (average three) — [IT Brief UK](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/ai-code-needs-production-debugging-lightrun-report-finds)
- Canary + kill switch: release to a small subset of users or servers, monitor, then promote; wrap risky code in an inverted flag so "off" is the safe default; kill switch triggers independently of the deployment pipeline (vendor) — [Unleash, reducing release risk](https://www.getunleash.io/blog/how-do-i-reduce-risk-associated-with-releasing-new-features)
- Financial services: "Without audit logs or approval workflows, there's no way to show an auditor who made a change, when it happened, or whether it was authorized"; mature organisations treat flag changes "as production changes with approval workflows, logs, and alerting on risky modifications" (vendor) — [CloudBees, feature flags for FS](https://webflow.cloudbees.com/blog/feature-flags-use-cases-for-financial-services); [FitGap, credit policy rollout](https://us.fitgap.com/stack-guides/controlled-rollout-and-rollback-for-credit-policy-changes-without-mass-customer-disruption)
- Change control: change authority may be an automated rule for low-risk changes; emergency changes via ECAB and retroactive documentation; post-implementation review — [itsm.tools](https://itsm.tools/change-enablement/); [InvGate](https://blog.invgate.com/emergency-change-control-process)
- Replay as regression gate: capture accepted behaviour, replay against the candidate, fail CI on response mismatch versus a preserved baseline (Speedscale's controlled check: a 24→25 total change produced one mismatch while HTTP still returned 200) — [Speedscale](https://speedscale.com/blog/definitive-guide-to-traffic-replay/)

### Inferences
- The artifact a change authority wants is evidence: a failing reproduction before the fix and a passing one after, plus a rollout/rollback plan (flag or canary). An agent that produces a reproducible failing test and the diff that fixes it supplies most of that evidence.

### Gaps
- No regulator or bank source stating what test evidence a CAB/ECAB requires for an emergency fix; ITIL sources are generic.
- No data on ephemeral-environment adoption in regulated firms.

## Q6. Documentation and closure: RCA/postmortem templates, problem records, linking fix and deployment

### Takeaway
The closing documents are the postmortem (timeline with cited data sources, impact, contributing factors, action tickets, external messaging), the ITIL problem/known-error record linked to incidents and the change, and, for EU financial entities, the DORA final report. Timelines are built from chat logs, monitoring, logs and deployments.

### Cited Findings
- Google SRE (book, c. 2016–2017): postmortem triggers are user-visible downtime/degradation beyond a threshold, any data loss, on-call intervention (rollback, traffic rerouting), resolution time above a threshold, monitoring failure; blameless: identify contributing causes "without indicting any individual or team"; a postmortem is "a written record of the incident, its impact, the actions taken to mitigate or resolve it, the root causes, and follow-up actions"; drafts are reviewed by senior engineers for completeness, impact, depth of root cause, action-plan priority and stakeholder communication — [Google SRE Book, Postmortem Culture](https://sre.google/sre-book/postmortem-culture/)
- PagerDuty template/process: Sev-1 meeting within 3 calendar days, Sev-2 within 5 business days; timeline records facts only, starting before the incident, drawn from "chat logs, monitoring, logs, and deployments", each entry citing a data source (metric, graph, log search, third-party page); impact = duration, customers affected, customers who contacted support, functionality affected measured by a business metric; analysis identifies contributing factors not a single root cause; follow-up tickets only P0/P1 in the owning team's project with severity/date labels, with reasons documented for actions not taken; external messaging (summary, what happened, what we are doing); link posted for review ≥24h before the meeting — [PagerDuty, Writing](https://postmortems.pagerduty.com/how_to_write/writing/)
- ITIL problem record contents and linkage: problem → root cause analysis (5 Whys, fishbone, fault tree) → known error (root cause and/or workaround documented) → linked to all related incidents and to the RFC; known errors may be accepted permanently if the fix's cost outweighs benefit; closure after the change is implemented and verified — [Atlassian problem management](https://www.atlassian.com/itsm/problem-management/process); [IT Process Maps checklist](https://wiki.en.it-processmaps.com/index.php/Checklist_Problem_Record); [ServiceNow problem management](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/yokohama/it-service-management/problem-management/c_ProblemManagementProcess.html)
- DORA final report due one month after the (latest) intermediate report; initial/intermediate/final structure per RTS 2025/301 — [Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/insights/ict-incident-management-classification-and-reporting-under-dora); [ESMA operational instructions](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-09/DORA_Incident_reporting_-_Operational_instructions.pdf)
- Datadog positions agent output as "audit-ready root cause analyses", including rejected alternatives — [Datadog](https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/building-bits-ai-sre/)

### Inferences
- The postmortem timeline is a mechanical merge of four sources (chat, alerts/monitoring, logs, deploy history) with citations — the most automatable document step. Root-cause depth and action prioritisation are the human review criteria.
- Linking fix commit → change record → incident/problem is done by reference fields in ITSM; sources describe the links but not an automated mechanism.

### Gaps
- Atlassian's template fields and severity-to-postmortem rules could not be fetched. The official ITIL 4 Problem Management practice guide text was not accessed.
- No source describing how firms link the deployed artifact version to the incident record automatically.

## Q7. Industry specifics shaping reproduction (airline PNR/GDS, banking/ISO 20022, telecom BSS/OSS)

### Takeaway
In all three sectors the triggering record lives in or passes through an external system that cannot be freely driven in test: the GDS/PSS (Sabre/Amadeus), the payment rail (Swift/instant payments with UETR), or the network/mediation chain producing CDRs. Reproduction therefore depends on test records in vendor sandboxes, correlation ids that survive the whole chain, and virtualised externals.

### Cited Findings
- Airline: Sabre separates Certification/Test and Production endpoints; CERT is for trying APIs, viewing response formats and sending/cancelling test PNRs; CERT data limited for cache-based REST APIs and missing some carriers; production test bookings incur supplier fees unless cancelled; Red Apps go CERT → Sabre-approved production beta — [Sabre, Environments](https://developer.sabre.com/guides/travel-agency/reference/environments); [Sabre, Red Apps and GDS environments](https://developer.sabre.com/red-workspace-and-gds-environments)
- Banking: UETR gives end-to-end visibility across stages; investigation cases keyed to a single transaction reference (camt.110/111); ISO 20022 message timing enables anomaly-based failure localisation; DORA timelines apply — [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments-general/swift-launches-case-management-for-cross-border-payment-investigations--1273210); [ISO 20022 CR](https://www.iso20022.org/milestone/25064/download); [arXiv 2510.21710](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21710v1); [Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/insights/ict-incident-management-classification-and-reporting-under-dora)

## Q8. What is already (semi-)automated, and the recurring sub-steps with the inputs an automated agent needs

### Takeaway
Vendors and in-house teams have automated alert-triggered investigation (telemetry queries, deploy correlation, hypothesis testing), traffic capture/replay with dependency mocking, masked test-data provisioning, and postmortem timeline assembly. Autonomous remediation is not yet accepted practice; humans keep approval, blast-radius and postmortem judgement. The inputs are consistently read-only observability access, change history, masked or recorded data, and a way to run the service against mocks.

### Cited Findings
- Automated investigation: Datadog Bits (automatic on alerts/incidents, hypothesis loop, claims "up to 95%" time-to-resolution reduction, no methodology given) — [Datadog](https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/building-bits-ai-sre/); Wix enriches 30,000+ alerts/month with claimed 90% root-cause accuracy, results in Slack in ~1 minute; ClickHouse's Aug 2025 experiment found four frontier LLMs with telemetry access did not reliably find four injected root causes without human guidance; models fixate and miss multi-cause incidents — [ClickHouse](https://clickhouse.com/resources/engineering/ai-sre-agents)
- 97% of engineering leaders believe AI SREs operate without significant visibility into production; 44% attribute failed automated investigations to missing execution-level data — [IT Brief UK / Lightrun](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/ai-code-needs-production-debugging-lightrun-report-finds)
- Automated reproduction of bug reports from natural-language steps is an active research area (Android, NLP + RL) — [arXiv 2301.07775](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.07775)
- Record/replay and virtual services are productised (Speedscale, GoReplay, Parasoft, Broadcom, WireMock) with PII masking as a precondition — [Speedscale](https://speedscale.com/blog/definitive-guide-to-traffic-replay/); [Parasoft](https://parasoft.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/case-study-telecom-service-virtualization.pdf)
- Postmortem timelines are assembled from chat, monitoring, logs and deployments with a data source per entry — [PagerDuty](https://postmortems.pagerduty.com/how_to_write/writing/)

### Inferences — recurring sub-steps and exact inputs for an automated agent
1. Triage/enrich the report. Inputs: the incident/bug record (title, description, severity, environment, timestamps, affected record identifier such as PNR/order id/UETR/MSISDN), service catalog/CMDB entry (owning team, dependencies), recent deploy and config-change history for the service. Output: completed fields, missing-information request to reporter.
2. Correlate telemetry for the record and the window. Inputs: read-only query access to logs, traces and metrics (Splunk/Elastic/Datadog/Dynatrace APIs or MCP), the correlation id(s) and time window, retention long enough for a before/after comparison. Output: trace of the failing request, implicated service(s), first bad timestamp.
3. Correlate with changes. Inputs: deployment history (version per service per time), feature-flag change log, infra/config change records. Output: candidate change(s) aligned with first bad timestamp.
4. Read the affected production record(s). Inputs: approved read-only path to a read replica or masked view, scoped to the record identifier, with audit logging; or a masked snapshot/subset produced by TDM for that record. Output: record state before/after.
5. Build a reproduction. Inputs: the service's source repository at the deployed commit, a runnable build (container image), a seeded database (masked subset or synthetic rows for the identifiers), recorded or synthesised responses from external dependencies (virtual services/mocks for GDS, bureau, payment rail, downstream services), the request(s) carrying the correlation id (recorded traffic or reconstructed from the trace). Output: a failing test or replay.
6. Diagnose and fix. Inputs: the repository, the failing reproduction, logs/stack traces from step 2. Output: patch plus a regression test.
7. Verify. Inputs: the reproduction from step 5 run against the patched build, replay baseline for unchanged behaviour, access to an integration/ephemeral environment. Output: before/after evidence for the change record.
8. Document. Inputs: chat transcript, alert history, log searches, deploy events (for the timeline), impact metrics, ticket system write access. Output: postmortem draft with cited timeline, contributing factors, proposed P0/P1 action items; problem/known-error record text; DORA intermediate/final report inputs where applicable.
9. Hand off for human judgement. Remediation approval, rollout/rollback decision, CAB/ECAB approval and postmortem review remain human per every source consulted.

### Gaps
- No independent accuracy benchmark for AI investigators beyond vendor self-reports and ClickHouse's small experiment.
- No source describing an enterprise that has automated the "read masked production record + stand up service with mocks" reproduction step end to end; the pieces (TDM, SV, replay) are separately productised.
